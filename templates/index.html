About this model

This is a Deep Convolutional GAN (DCGAN) — a system of two neural networks trained in opposition. The generator learns to produce anime faces from random noise vectors. The discriminator learns to tell real training images apart from generated ones. Neither can win permanently — as the discriminator improves, the generator is forced to improve too, and vice versa.

The generator maps a 128-dimensional noise vector through four transposed convolution layers, upsampling from a 4×4 feature map up to a 64×64 RGB image. Every face generated is unique — it has never existed before and is not stored anywhere.

Limitations

  • ResolutionAll outputs are 64×64 pixels. Fine details — eyelashes, sharp hair strands, complex expressions — are not representable at this resolution regardless of training quality.
  • D dominanceThe discriminator gradually outpaced the generator after epoch ~50. D loss fell to 0.59 and G loss rose to 3.2 by epoch 100, meaning the generator received weaker gradient signal in late training. At 64×64 resolution the visual impact is limited — the full epoch 100 model is deployed as it produces preferred results despite the loss divergence.
  • Dataset sizeOnly half the available dataset (33,180 of 63,565 images) was loaded during training due to a Colab cache issue. Less variety in training data means less variety in generated faces.
  • ArchitectureDCGAN is a foundational architecture from 2015. It has a hard quality ceiling compared to modern generative models trained at higher resolutions.

How to improve this model

  • Full datasetTrain on all 63,565 images instead of 33,180. More variety means the discriminator cannot memorise patterns as easily, extending the competitive phase of training.
  • More G updatesUpdate the generator twice per discriminator update. When the discriminator pulls ahead, the generator gets more gradient steps to catch up — directly countering dominance.
  • Lower D lrHalve the discriminator learning rate again (0.0001 → 0.00005). Slows the discriminator further in later epochs without changing the architecture.
  • Spectral normAdd spectral normalisation to the discriminator layers. Constrains gradient magnitudes so the discriminator cannot dominate regardless of learning rate.
  • 128×128Add one more upsampling block to the generator and one more downsampling block to the discriminator. Doubles the output resolution — significantly more detail at the cost of longer training.
  • StyleGAN2Switch to the StyleGAN2 architecture — purpose-built for face generation, produces near-photorealistic results. Significantly more complex to implement, but pretrained checkpoints on this exact dataset are publicly available.
